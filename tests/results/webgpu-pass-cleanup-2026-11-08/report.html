WebGPU timestamps and pass/format cleanup

Only the two authorized items were implemented. Other research leads are ranked in the backlog. This is a conservative execution cleanup, not a demonstrated whole-scene performance win.

What changed

  • Optional GPU timestamps on rendering and connected compute passes. Two bounded asynchronous readback slots, at most 256 pass pairs per submission, no new queue wait. Busy slots drop diagnostics instead of delaying execution. Allocation/mapping failures disable profiling only. Samples retain submission IDs; unsupported devices report unavailable.
  • Per-pass costs, their sum and enclosing GPU span are separate. Passes may overlap or reorder; their sum is not frame duration. Counter resets and implausible spans are rejected. See wgpu timestamp semantics and WebGPU timing/query specification.
  • Consecutive compatible draws share render passes, and a preceding clear becomes the pass load operation. Uploads, copies, target changes and later clears remain boundaries. Draw order, viewport/scissor, depth and blending state remain intact.
  • R/RG data textures retain native channel counts rather than expanding to RGBA. All values keep their original precision; full-precision HDR, hit records and render attachments are unchanged. No half-precision conversion or attachment discard was added.

Actual complete-beach measurements

Codex in-app browser on this Mac, AMD GCN-4. Shared full scene at 320 × 200, 1,164 water samples, wind 24, three boulders. CPU/WASM water stepping in both arms; GPU rendering and existing GPU auxiliary systems remain active. Each A/B/B/A block resets the physical workload, warms eight frames and measures 24 frames with one fixed 1/15-second step per frame. Both arms use the same look and physics. Presentation remains capped in production; this bounded test is below 60 FPS. These results do not measure high-resolution production performance or RTX hardware.

Image and numerical validation

  • Forced full redraws, baseline → cleanup → baseline: exact Float32 pixel equality (maximum and mean error 0) for beach at 320 × 200, four objects + carved sand at 160 × 100, and underwater night at 160 × 100. Paused image caching was explicitly bypassed. Pass counts: beach 30→25; objects 29→24; underwater 37→31. These are frozen tests, not long-running feature validation.
  • Tiny real-GPU R32/RG32 sampling test preserved channel defaults, blending-independent order and scissor restoration. Exact pixel equality; passes 4→2.
  • Seven connected compute cases passed against CPU references: open fields at 48/140/540 particles, carved terrain, boundaries, player contact and a sealed field. Overflow guard passed; actual 1,164-particle beach warm-up passed. No solver equations or physical behavior changed. Final numerical/timing results.
  • 50 focused unit/numerical tests and npm run build passed. Includes pass barriers, full-precision formats, unsupported timing, bounded slots, out-of-order completion, counter resets, overlapping passes, query capacity and profiling allocation failure.

Frozen feature image comparisons. That early capture used summed pass costs in an initial diagnostic guard; those old timing fields are not GPU frame duration. Final code and compute capture distinguish sum from span.

Limits and next leads

A 320 × 200 four-object/rain/edit baseline frame exceeded the existing two-second guard and the test stopped/disposed its device. No timeout was raised and no retry loop was added. Subsequent tiny, beach, compute and reduced feature tests worked without browser or machine recovery. Heavy feature playback remains unverified; the smaller frozen tests do not resolve that limitation.

Two warmed cleanup render captures showed opaque hit tracing at 7.3–7.5 ms, density reconstruction ~7.1 ms, medium lighting 4.8–5.4 ms and fine-water transport ~3.5 ms. One warmed connected-GPU job spent 27.1 of 31.8 ms in pressure. These are leads, not promised speedups or representative averages. Next experiments should address those costs and CPU/GPU water handoffs, with numerical and full-scene gates. Half precision, attachment discard, resident-state restructuring, topology reuse and multi-workgroup pressure remain backlog work.

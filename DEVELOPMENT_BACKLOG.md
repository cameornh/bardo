# Development backlog

Updated: 2026-11-07. This is working memory for future development, not a promise to implement every idea. Read alongside `AGENTS.md`. Keep the highest-value next experiment near the top; record evidence before promoting an optimization. Remove obsolete leads rather than accumulating permanent wish lists.

## Constraints and preferences to preserve

- Stability first: bounded workloads, sequential first-use compilation, one outstanding compute/frame job, finite timeouts, explicit CPU fallback. Do not ask the user to restart the machine or repeatedly run diagnostics we can run ourselves.
- Keep `main` and `codex/rtx-5070-ti-gpu` in feature parity. Machine policy may differ; experience and controls must not. Leave historical test implementations isolated and preserve saved terrain/clay.
- Cap presentation at 60 FPS; spend capacity on simulation speed and difficult views. At simulation speed 1, do not exceed 1.00× realtime.
- Use real covariance-projected Gaussians and SH environment response. Physics must not depend on the camera. World resolution is relative to the constant-size player; enlarging the beach adds cells, not larger cells. Boulders keep their size and spacing.
- The user likes sparse, noisy rendering and tolerates localized flicker. Avoid full-screen flicker, exposure flashes, abrupt LOD bands, or camera-driven simulation changes. Preserve the cohesive visual appearance.
- Keep controls functional. Let the user lead feature selection; suggestions below are optional, not authorization for unrelated feature work.

## Priority 0 — correctness and stability

1. **Long-running regression coverage** — pending broader validation. Run bounded, checkpointed sequences of wind, rain, repeated sand grab/drop/carve, object spawning, camera rotation, underwater views and day/lunar changes. Track FPS, simulation/wall time, p95/p99 latency, live particle counts, shape/cache counts, allocations and program count. Short successful runs do not resolve the history of gradual slowdowns or first-use driver failures. Stop on the first unhealthy result; no automatic retry storm.
2. **Field/boundary invariants** — retain as release gates. Resting water and shore edges must stay stable; deposition/projection and surface corrections must not inject energy at walls. Track volume, divergence, momentum and CPU/GPU differences. Recheck carved contacts, oriented cups, player contacts and fallback after reset when changing transfer/topology.
3. **Rendering regression pack** — extend existing fixtures with frozen-state image comparisons at silhouettes, glass over sand, underwater black glass/bubbles/condensation, lamps near the horizon, and camera motion. Test optical-layer order and scattering, not just “no shader error.” New particle culling must never use water/glass as opaque blockers or remove off-screen reflection contributors.

## Priority 1 — measured performance work

4. **Sand/foam follow-up** — first pass completed. Live frustum rejection, optional conservative opaque-depth rejection, shared SH irradiance, one-target fine lighting and stable sparse drawing are implemented. Busy above-water particle passes improve substantially, but repeated complete-frame results do not establish an overall FPS gain. Occlusion defaults off because it costs more here. Preserve this baseline while profiling dominant whole-frame CPU work and testing active-list compaction. [Images, timings and limitations](tests/results/particle-visibility-2026-11-07/report.html).
5. **GPU-resident simulation boundary** — implemented experimentally, not yet a default win. Deposition, pressure, projection, transfer, neighbor search and contacts now share one WebGPU submission/readback; grid connectivity is cached. Remaining CPU prediction, occupancy/bin membership, geometry/topology, scalar transport and WebGL upload still create boundaries. Profile those boundaries before moving the next connected group. On this Mac at 13,293 water particles, complete physics medians were CPU 138.0 ms, particle-only WebGPU 110.8 ms, connected WebGPU 143.0 ms. Keep the CPU default until matched rendered tests show a reliable improvement. [Evidence](tests/results/connected-fields-2026-11-07/report.html).
6. **Parallel pressure execution** — untested follow-up, not another solver rewrite by default. Multigrid reduced GPU pressure from roughly 54–58 iterations to 7–9 but serial workgroup sweeps remained costly. Compare multiple-workgroup smoothing/reduction, bounded dispatch batches, and cheaper hierarchy updates. Include synchronization/dispatch cost and true residual, not just iteration count.
7. **Terrain editing cost over time** — extend the existing incremental geometry/collision cache work. Measure dirty-region rebuilds and cache growth after hundreds of repeated grab/drop/carve operations. Consolidate settled shapes without erasing bowls, overhangs, material wetness or mass. Contact meshes are an acceleration structure; exact SDF fallback still matters near modified geometry.
8. **RTX measurements** — pending access to actual test evidence, not a predicted speedup. Run the same frozen scenes and complete-physics workloads on Windows/RTX 5070 Ti when available. Record browser/ANGLE backend and warm-up. Low total CPU/GPU utilization alone does not identify the bottleneck; individual cores, queue latency, transfer and serialized passes can dominate.

## Priority 2 — promising experiments, not yet established wins

9. **Screen-space fluid reconstruction with Gaussians** — isolated prototype. Covariance-projected depth/thickness splats, separable depth-aware/narrow-range filtering, world-space smoothing radii, lower-resolution thickness. Start with fine spray/distant water. A single front-depth layer cannot replace underwater or multi-layer refraction. Compare against current field tracing. The three video transcripts are already reviewed in the [particle compute report](tests/results/particle-compute-2026-11-07/report.html); revisit the recorded timestamps instead of summarizing the titles again.
10. **Visibility compaction / active marker lists** — distinguish from cheap shader rejection. GPU compaction or conservative spatial groups can reduce vertex invocations for dead/hidden slots. Preserve blending order where needed and avoid synchronous visibility readbacks. Only worth adding if saved work exceeds the scans, buffers and transfers.
11. **Shared receiver data and lighting** — partial deferred shading already exists. Reuse depth/normal/material and bounded light lists for opaque receivers; avoid repeated SDF/medium integration. Transparent water, glass and secondary rays still need specialized paths. Keep shaders small; do not recreate the old monolithic compiler workload.
12. **Stable stochastic rendering** — per-particle ID thinning and opacity compensation are now implemented for fine water and small grains. Future experiments could extend it to distant surface reconstruction or per-cell coverage before attempting full temporal stochastic transparency. Measure overdraw and noise at fixed brightness. Do not jitter the entire camera, drive exposure with sampling noise, or alter physics to match visual thinning.
13. **Stronger continuous visual LOD** — preserve nearby shore detail; coarsen distant/interior rendering smoothly. Audit existing half-resolution soft-particle rendering before adding more resolution levels. Hardware variable-rate shading is not assumed available through the current browser APIs; software multi-resolution passes are the practical starting point.
14. **A WebGPU rendering bridge** — longer-term experiment if profiling proves cross-API transfers dominate. Keep particle/field data resident through drawing; retain a WebGL/CPU path and branch parity. A native executable would not by itself cure large shaders, excess work, or driver failures. Do not migrate the engine without a measured prototype.

## User-directed feature directions to retain

These are ongoing intentions and regression requirements. Historical requests are not automatically reopened bugs.

- **Generalized material properties:** continuous wetness-dependent cohesion, adhesion, yielding, settling, drag and particulate emission rather than dry/wet behavior tiers. Previously wet sand retains some shape memory as it dries; stress can break unsupported structures. New materials should eventually be property variations, not new hardcoded behavior trees. Existing continuous sand work is a starting point, not a finished general material system.
- **Coupled air/water fields:** pressure, velocity, water/air temperature and air humidity, with debug views. Fields and experimental GPU coupling exist; broader external effects remain future work. Evaporation, condensation from humidity, heat-driven motion and clouds require a separate user-directed scope. Currently condensation is rain-generated surface water, not atmospheric phase change.
- **Rain/condensation:** beads on objects/boulders only, never sand/player/water; remove beads on submersion. Rain water may collect on those hard receivers or enter water directly, not generate rolling blobs on sand. Preserve small round beads, reduced opacity, glass/water tint, object-surface impacts, airborne lingering mist, rain-only horizontal wrapping, and prompt start/stop response.
- **Sea foam:** organic-load dependent (none in clean water), yellower with more organics, bounded persistent patches/filaments concentrated at and ahead of the shore, advected by waves and stranded on retreating water. Do not turn the surface into a uniform white carpet.
- **Objects/player:** carved terrain and cup-to-cup contacts, natural angular response with water damping, external forces on the player at restrained strength, reliable first-person fallback and floating with the eyes above water. Keep the preferred spontaneous large bubbles, at the requested smaller size.
- **Sky/lighting:** independent solar and lunar controls, phase/tide behavior, star visibility through exposure without excessive moon suppression, straight Milky Way, stationary star centers with stronger subtle twinkle on smaller stars. Direct-light control affects moonlight too. Maintain underwater scattering and avoid horizon illumination artifacts/global flashes.
- **Intentional edge mirage:** retain actual bounded grazing-ray behavior and strength control; do not replace it with uniform silhouette thinning. Zero disables the effect.
- **Water-test fixtures:** ornate shiny multi-basin fountain, strong adjustable jets with mist/whitewater to their apex, coherent laminar pipe stream. Preserve snapshots; port additional fixtures to the shared live renderer only as a deliberate task.

## Optional product/development suggestions

- A small saved workload suite (calm shore, storm, rain on glass, edited terrain, busy objects) with per-stage timing and before/after image export would make future tuning more reliable. Keep it behind QA controls.
- A future material parameter inspector would make the generalized-property direction easier to tune. Avoid building a broad editor until the underlying properties are consistent and the user wants it.

## Evidence and maintenance

- Current compute baseline: [connected fields](tests/results/connected-fields-2026-11-07/report.html); [particle compute, video transcript review](tests/results/particle-compute-2026-11-07/report.html); [earlier field pass](tests/results/field-performance-2026-11-06/report.md).
- Instancing, split surface tracing/shading, half-resolution soft water particles, CPU multigrid and optional connected GPU compute already exist. Do not relist them as wholly unimplemented ideas.
- Rejected particle prototypes: a full-screen max-depth hierarchy, an extra grain preparation pass, and covariance projection moved into the fine-lighting pass were slower. Keep them rejected until a different workload provides evidence. Current raw-depth occlusion is conservative and optional. Audit signed `pow(x, 2)` in future live shader changes; use `x*x` for signed covariance terms without silently modifying historical snapshots.
- When finishing a task: update status, record what was measured and on which machine, link the result, and remove disproven/replaced leads. Keep rejected prototypes' lesson if it prevents repeating expensive work. Never present a numerical microbenchmark as full-scene FPS or simulation throughput.

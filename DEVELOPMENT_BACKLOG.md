# Development backlog

Updated: 2026-11-08. This is working memory for future development, not a promise to implement every idea. Read alongside `AGENTS.md`. Keep the highest-value next experiment near the top; record evidence before promoting an optimization. Remove obsolete leads rather than accumulating permanent wish lists.

## Constraints and preferences to preserve

- Stability first: bounded workloads, sequential first-use compilation, one outstanding compute/frame job, finite timeouts, explicit CPU fallback. Do not ask the user to restart the machine or repeatedly run diagnostics we can run ourselves.
- Keep `main` and `codex/rtx-5070-ti-gpu` in feature parity. Machine policy may differ; experience and controls must not. Leave historical test implementations isolated and preserve saved terrain/clay.
- Cap presentation at 60 FPS; spend capacity on simulation speed and difficult views. At simulation speed 1, do not exceed 1.00× realtime.
- Use real covariance-projected Gaussians and SH environment response. Physics must not depend on the camera. World resolution is relative to the constant-size player; enlarging the beach adds cells, not larger cells. Boulders keep their size and spacing.
- The user likes sparse, noisy rendering and tolerates localized flicker. Avoid full-screen flicker, exposure flashes, abrupt LOD bands, or camera-driven simulation changes. Preserve the cohesive visual appearance.
- Keep controls functional. Let the user lead feature selection; suggestions below are optional, not authorization for unrelated feature work.

## Priority 2 — promising experiments, not yet established wins

10. **Screen-space fluid reconstruction with Gaussians** — implemented in the isolated resident-water prototype. True covariance-projected Gaussian depth/thickness, eight small bilateral filter draws, SH environment lighting and attenuated scattering preserve the broad beach/water appearance. Conservative frustum rejection fixes huge off-axis ellipses at low camera angles. Remaining: softer surface, diagonal transmission transition at the bounded pool's far edge, missing glass/underwater layers/arbitrary reflections and SDF edge mirage. These block visual parity; keep the live renderer until addressed. Next measure compute filtering or lower-resolution thickness, not more cross-API passes. The three video transcripts are already reviewed in the [particle compute report](tests/results/particle-compute-2026-11-07/report.html).
11. **Visibility compaction / active marker lists** — distinguish from cheap shader rejection. GPU compaction or conservative spatial groups can reduce vertex invocations for dead/hidden slots. Preserve blending order where needed and avoid synchronous visibility readbacks. Only worth adding if saved work exceeds the scans, buffers and transfers.
12. **Shared receiver data and lighting** — partial deferred shading already exists. Reuse depth/normal/material and bounded light lists for opaque receivers; avoid repeated SDF/medium integration. Transparent water, glass and secondary rays still need specialized paths. Keep shaders small; do not recreate the old monolithic compiler workload.
13. **Stable stochastic rendering** — per-particle ID thinning and opacity compensation are now implemented for fine water and small grains. Future experiments could extend it to distant surface reconstruction or per-cell coverage before attempting full temporal stochastic transparency. Measure overdraw and noise at fixed brightness. Do not jitter the entire camera, drive exposure with sampling noise, or alter physics to match visual thinning.
14. **Stronger continuous visual LOD** — preserve nearby shore detail; coarsen distant/interior rendering smoothly. Audit existing half-resolution soft-particle rendering before adding more resolution levels. Hardware variable-rate shading is not assumed available through the current browser APIs; software multi-resolution passes are the practical starting point.
15. **WebGPU migration policy** — both the fast reduced resident-water prototype and the faithful full-scene native port exist; see item 6. The full port is opt-in via `renderer=webgpu`; its slower results do not justify changing defaults. Keep both branches on the same features and retain the current CPU/WebGL route. Validate the full feature workload, not only the reduced calm beach, before selecting defaults. Native Vulkan remains on the backburner per the user; a native executable would not itself cure shader size, excess work or driver failures.

## Research queue — 2026-11-08

Current authorized scope: **try focused experiments from this queue**, following timestamps and pass/format cleanup. This pass tests fixed-neighbor reuse and coarse-grid workgroup memory; native wgpu is planning only. Full-frame FPS, realtime speed, p95 latency and visual/numerical parity are the acceptance criteria. Legacy submission timings include queue waits and must not be called shader execution time. Native WebGPU now reports asynchronous per-pass timestamps, their sum and enclosing span separately; passes may overlap.

1. **GPU pass timestamps — implemented and numerically verified.** Optional `timestamp-query`, bounded pooled asynchronous readback, render and connected compute pass labels. Report unsupported devices explicitly. Do not add a synchronous per-pass fence or change the one-outstanding-frame policy. [WebGPU timing](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-121).
2. **Render-pass and format cleanup — first conservative pass complete.** Preserve command ordering and texture hazards; group compatible consecutive draws and first-draw clears. Start with exact native R/RG data formats; keep depth-bearing alpha, world coordinates, pressure and faint HDR stars at their current precision. Selective half precision needs separate image evidence; attachment discard requires proven last use. [WebGPU load/store](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/), [AMD precision guidance](https://gpuopen.com/learn/first-steps-implementing-fp16/).
   **Evidence:** 49 → 40 render passes in beach playback (18% fewer); frozen beach, objects/edited terrain and underwater night outputs are pixel-identical. Net comparison including new timestamp overhead: 8.05 → 8.03 FPS and 0.536× → 0.535× realtime at 320 × 200 / 1,164 markers. No established whole-scene speedup. Fifty numerical/unit tests, seven real-GPU connected solver cases and build pass. The 320 × 200 busy baseline hit the existing two-second frame guard; smaller frozen feature checks passed, so heavier playback remains unverified. [Measurements and limits](tests/results/webgpu-pass-cleanup-2026-11-08/report.html).
   **New profiling leads:** two warmed render captures put opaque hit tracing at 7.3–7.5 ms, density reconstruction at ~7.1 ms and medium lighting at 4.8–5.4 ms. A warmed connected-GPU job spent 27.1 of 31.8 ms in pressure; prioritize measuring multi-workgroup pressure on that path before increasing grid size. These are diagnostic samples, not end-to-end speedup predictions. Retain current CPU fallback. Native channel cleanup does not yet establish a whole-scene memory win; half precision/discard remain pending.
3. **Authoritative GPU water state — highest structural payoff, pending.** Keep particle/grid/contact/transport/reconstruction buffers resident. Replace full-state CPU readback with sparse edit/emission commands and small body-force/debug results. Audit every CPU consumer, including scalar chemistry, rain, player and sand coupling. Delayed rigid forces must not introduce unstable buoyancy. This extends item 6, not another isolated bridge.
4. **Reusable grid structure and local invalidation — fixed-neighbor reuse implemented; broader invalidation pending.** Cache only spatial adjacency and use collision-free numeric aggregation keys. Rebuild phase-dependent coefficients, sealed components, gauges and coarse operators; fresh hierarchy identities invalidate existing GPU/WASM coefficient caches. Matrix, hierarchy, gauge and solution comparisons are bit-identical across changing masks. At 3,553 cells, the isolated topology rebuild fell from 8.18 to 2.48 ms; this is not a whole-scene speedup claim. CPU full-frame results were flat, despite reduced physics time. Next test dirty-brick terrain uploads and phase-aware hierarchy reuse without stale coefficients; original capture had 47 topology uploads across 45 frames (multiple steps possible). [Parallel multigrid](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~sifakis/project_pages/mgpcg.html).
5. **Parallel fine-grid pressure execution — fine-grid parallelism pending; coarse locality implemented.** Current connected pressure still uses one workgroup. Small-level smoothing now keeps iterates in bounded workgroup memory, preserving precision, iteration counts and convergence gates. Validated GPU pressure timestamps fell 28–33% in two longer runs; actual FPS/realtime improved 8–13% with topology reuse. No extra dispatch/readback or looser solver tolerance. Fine-grid dispatches/reductions can spread work across the GPU; coarse levels should stay local. Workgroup barriers are not global barriers. Extra dispatch cost may lose on the current ~3,553-cell grid. Retain residual/divergence gates. Extends item 7.
6. **Stable GPU active-list compaction — pending.** Scan alive/visible IDs and draw indirectly; keep stable IDs and required alpha order. Separate reflection visibility and leave physics camera-independent. Saves dead/hidden vertex/light work, not visible fragment overdraw. Previous Hi-Z attempt lost; do not silently repeat it. [Scan/compaction reference](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-vi-gpu-computing/chapter-39-parallel-prefix-sum-scan-cuda).
7. **Narrow-range screen-space reconstruction — later experiment.** Compare with the resident prototype's eight bilateral passes; use actual Gaussian-projected depth, possibly tiled compute. Retain world-space data for hidden surfaces, glass, underwater paths, reflections and intentional SDF mirage. Do not claim reduced-prototype throughput as full-scene parity. [Truong/Yuksel filter and code](https://ttnghia.github.io/posts/narrow-range-filter/).
8. **Narrow-band fluid physics — longer-term.** Particles near the free surface, grid-only deep interior. Requires level/surface tracking, reseeding and conservative transport of temperature/tannins plus correct cups/shore/thin-stream handling. Naive particle/grid velocity blending can inject energy. Band follows the water surface, never the camera. Our solver is not stock FLIP: this is a model adaptation. [Ferstl et al.](https://visualcomputing.ist.ac.at/publications/2016/NarrowBandFLIP/).
9. **Native wgpu host — requested backlog plan, not started; after browser bottlenecks are measured.** Reuse WGSL kernels, resource layouts and pass descriptions through one wgpu implementation targeting Metal on Mac and DirectX 12 or Vulkan on Windows. Keep the browser WebGPU route and CPU reference. First run one identical bounded pressure/render workload in a minimal native host and compare startup, images, residuals, complete-frame time and device-loss handling on both machines; no full engine port unless those results justify it. Then extract a platform-neutral command/resource layer, add native input/window/control plumbing, assets and saved-terrain compatibility, packaging and shader hot reload. Existing JavaScript simulation/UI does not become native automatically: evaluate a small bridge versus moving its CPU kernels into a reusable Rust/WASM core before committing to migration. Avoid duplicating gameplay/features across hosts. Preserve Gaussian/SH rendering, all live controls, the 60 FPS cap, CPU fallback and strict workload budgets. Native access can change driver/translation overhead; it cannot remove excess work, bad shader structure or guarantee freedom from GPU crashes. Use this portable path before considering a separate Vulkan-only engine. [Official wgpu platforms and bindings](https://wgpu.rs/).

Avoid prioritizing render bundles while CPU encoding is only ~1–2 ms, pressure-only rewrites while CPU projection is small, or more queued frames/removing watchdogs to hide serialized work. Sparse grid papers (SPGrid/GVDB) offer locality ideas, not browser-ready replacements; their native virtual-memory/CUDA assumptions do not transfer directly.

## User-directed feature directions to retain

These are ongoing intentions and regression requirements. Historical requests are not automatically reopened bugs.

- **Generalized material properties:** continuous wetness-dependent cohesion, adhesion, yielding, settling, drag and particulate emission rather than dry/wet behavior tiers. Previously wet sand retains some shape memory as it dries; stress can break unsupported structures. New materials should eventually be property variations, not new hardcoded behavior trees. Existing continuous sand work is a starting point, not a finished general material system.
- **Coupled air/water fields:** pressure, velocity, water/air temperature and air humidity, with debug views. Fields and experimental GPU coupling exist; broader external effects remain future work. Evaporation, condensation from humidity, heat-driven motion and clouds require a separate user-directed scope. Currently condensation is rain-generated surface water, not atmospheric phase change.
- **Rain/condensation:** beads on objects/boulders only, never sand/player/water; remove beads on submersion. Rain water may collect on those hard receivers or enter water directly, not generate rolling blobs on sand. Preserve small round beads, reduced opacity, glass/water tint, object-surface impacts, airborne lingering mist, rain-only horizontal wrapping, and prompt start/stop response.
- **Sea foam:** organic-load dependent (none in clean water), yellower with more organics, bounded persistent patches/filaments concentrated at and ahead of the shore, advected by waves and stranded on retreating water. Do not turn the surface into a uniform white carpet.
- **Objects/player:** carved terrain and cup-to-cup contacts, natural angular response with water damping, external forces on the player at restrained strength, reliable first-person fallback and floating with the eyes above water. Keep the preferred spontaneous large bubbles, at the requested smaller size.
- **Sky/lighting:** independent solar and lunar controls, phase/tide behavior, star visibility through exposure without excessive moon suppression, straight Milky Way, stationary star centers with stronger subtle twinkle on smaller stars. Direct-light control affects moonlight too. Maintain underwater scattering and avoid horizon illumination artifacts/global flashes.
- **Intentional edge mirage:** retain actual bounded grazing-ray behavior and strength control; do not replace it with uniform silhouette thinning. Zero disables the effect.
- **Water-test fixtures:** ornate shiny multi-basin fountain, strong adjustable jets with mist/whitewater to their apex, coherent laminar pipe stream. Preserve snapshots; port additional fixtures to the shared live renderer only as a deliberate task.

## Optional product/development suggestions

- A small saved workload suite (calm shore, storm, rain on glass, edited terrain, busy objects) with per-stage timing and before/after image export would make future tuning more reliable. Keep it behind QA controls.
- A future material parameter inspector would make the generalized-property direction easier to tune. Avoid building a broad editor until the underlying properties are consistent and the user wants it.
